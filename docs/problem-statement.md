# Problem Statement

## Background

India's major ports — JNPA/Nhava Sheva, Mundra, Chennai, Kandla, and Visakhapatnam — collectively handle over 1,400 million tonnes of cargo annually and are critical nodes in global maritime supply chains. Port operations are complex, time-sensitive, and highly interdependent: vessel arrivals, berth availability, crane allocation, and tidal windows must align precisely to keep traffic flowing.

## The Problem

Port operators and shift supervisors typically manage congestion reactively — they become aware of a developing queue only after vessels have already anchored and delays have compounded. Historical data shows that vessel waiting times at Indian major ports regularly exceed 24 hours during peak seasons, and avoidable congestion costs the Indian shipping industry an estimated ₹3,000–5,000 crore annually in demurrage charges, idle crane time, and fuel burn from waiting vessels.

There is no unified system that:
1. Forecasts congestion pressure **before** it materialises
2. Explains **why** the risk is elevated
3. Helps operators **act** — by optimising schedules and generating executable plans

## Who is Affected

**Port Operations Supervisors and Shift Managers** at major Indian port trusts (JNPA, Adani Ports Mundra, Chennai Port Authority, Kandla Port, Visakhapatnam Port Authority) who are responsible for real-time berth assignment, vessel prioritisation, and crane deployment. These individuals make high-stakes decisions under time pressure with fragmented information from multiple siloed systems.

Secondary stakeholders include **shipping agents**, **freight forwarders**, and **logistics planners** who need reliable ETA and congestion windows to coordinate land-side operations.

## Why It Matters

- **Financial cost:** A single vessel delayed 24 hours incurs demurrage charges of $15,000–$30,000 depending on vessel class. At a port like JNPA handling 80+ vessel calls per month, preventable congestion has significant direct cost.
- **Supply chain ripple effects:** Delays at major transhipment hubs cascade into missed connections, rail/truck scheduling disruptions, and delayed cargo clearance.
- **Environmental cost:** Vessels at anchor with engines running contribute to coastal air pollution — reducing anchorage time directly reduces port-area emissions.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Current port management systems (VTS, PMS) are operational — they track what is happening now. They do not forecast what will happen, explain why congestion is building, or suggest corrective actions. Manual planning processes rely on supervisor experience and are not scalable. No widely deployed system integrates predictive ML, live AIS vessel tracking, constraint-based schedule optimisation, and an AI assistant into a single operational workflow for Indian ports.
